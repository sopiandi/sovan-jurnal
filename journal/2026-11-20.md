# November 20, 2026 — How a Price List Saves Time at the Counter

Every small shop has the same busy moment: a line of customers, a hand reaching for change, and someone asking, "How much is this one?" If the seller has to stop and think, the whole line slows down. A simple price list fixes that. It is just a sheet — paper on the wall or a page in a notebook — with each item and its price written clearly.

The benefits are practical. First, speed: the seller reads instead of remembering, so each sale takes seconds. Second, consistency: every customer pays the same price, which builds trust. Third, training: a helper or a family member can serve customers without asking the owner about every price.

A good price list stays short and honest. Group similar items together, use round numbers where possible, and update it the moment a price changes — an outdated list causes more arguments than no list at all. It does not need fancy design or software. For a small business, the cheapest tool is often the most reliable one: a clear list, kept where the seller can see it, turns a rush of customers into a calm, steady routine.

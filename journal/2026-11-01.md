# November 1, 2026 — Digital Village Elections

Today I learned something new: village head elections in West Java,
Indonesia are starting to be held digitally (e-voting).

Notes from what I learned today:

- Voters still come to the polling station, bringing an invitation
  letter that carries a QR code.
- The code on the invitation is single-use. Once it has been used to
  vote, the system locks it so it cannot be used a second time.
- The voting booth has a tablet instead of paper ballots. The voter
  scans the invitation code, then picks a candidate by tapping the
  candidate's photo on the screen.
- Before the final confirmation, the choice can still be changed.
  After confirmation, the vote is stored permanently.
- The machine prints a receipt showing the voter has cast a vote,
  and the receipt goes into an audit box.
- Because the system counts the votes, results are known much faster
  than with manual counting.

General lesson: technology does not remove the need for oversight,
but it makes fraud harder to hide, because every step leaves a trail
that can be checked again later.

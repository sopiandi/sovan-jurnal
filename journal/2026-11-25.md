# November 25, 2026 — Why a Receipt Matters Even for a Small Purchase

A receipt looks like a scrap of paper, but it is a record. It says what was bought, how many, at what price, and when. For a small shop, that record is the memory of the business. Without it, a disagreement about a price has no evidence, and the owner has to rely on remembering hundreds of small sales a day.

For the buyer, a receipt is proof too. If something turns out to be wrong — the wrong item, a damaged pack, a price that does not match the shelf — the receipt is what makes an exchange or a refund simple. Without it, both sides are guessing.

Receipts also help a shop owner see patterns. At the end of the week, they show which items move fast and which ones sit on the shelf. That is how a shop decides what to order more of and what to stop stocking.

Keeping receipts does not need a fancy system. A notebook, a printed slip, or a simple app all do the same job: they turn a sale that would be forgotten into a fact that can be checked later.

# November 22, 2026 — What a QR Code Actually Stores

A QR code is a small square of black and white blocks, but it is really just a shortcut. Inside those blocks is a short piece of text — most often a web address, a payment link, or a Wi-Fi password. When your phone camera reads it, the phone simply opens whatever that text points to, saving you from typing a long address by hand.

The pattern is built to survive damage. Part of the square holds the message itself, and part of it holds backup copies of that message, so a code with a scratch or a coffee stain can often still be read. Three large squares in the corners tell the camera which way is up, which is why a QR code works even when it is tilted or upside down.

Because a QR code can point anywhere, it is worth a second of caution: check the address your phone shows before you tap it, just as you would check a link in a message. Used carefully, it is one of the simplest ideas in everyday technology — a printed link you can read with a camera.

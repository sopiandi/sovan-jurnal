# November 18, 2026 - Why a Small Shop Counts Its Cash Every Day

A cash drawer looks simple: money goes in, change comes out. But
without a daily count, small mistakes hide and grow. A miscounted
bill, a forgotten expense, or a sale recorded twice all look the
same at the end of the week — money that is there, or money that
is not, with no way to say why.

Counting the cash at the end of each day does three useful things:

- It catches errors while they are still fresh and easy to explain.
- It shows the real daily income, not a guess made a week later.
- It keeps a clear record, so the owner always knows what the
  shop actually earned.

The count takes only a few minutes: add up the cash, compare it
with the day's sales, and write down any difference. A small gap
found today is a five-minute question. The same gap found next
month is a mystery nobody can solve. Daily counting turns the
till from a black box into a habit the owner can trust.
